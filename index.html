Projects

Final-Year Project — MEMS Gas Sensing for Methane & Hydrogen Detection

2025 – 2026
University of Warwick · Supervised by Prof. Julian Gardner · In partnership with Flusso Ltd

An investigation into thermal conductivity sensor technology for detecting trace methane and hydrogen mixtures in air (0–1.2% Vol) — gases central to industrial safety, emissions tracking, and the UK's hydrogen-grid ambitions. Evaluated a MEMS thermal conductivity sensor based on the Flusso FLS310 IC, building a complete experimental platform: a custom PCB with differential amplification, a controlled gas-flow rig, and Arduino-based data acquisition over I²C.

Characterised sensor response across humidity and ambient temperature profiles, applied signal processing and data smoothing, and developed a localised multi-variable linear regression model compensating for environmental factors — achieving R² = 0.969 with an RMSE of 0.165 Ω, demonstrating proof of concept for a low-cost alternative to optical and electrochemical sensors.
